DermApIxel.

Evaluation of foundation models for clinical dermatology.

A homogeneous and independent evaluation of up to fourteen foundation models with public weights — with PanDerm and DermLIP as references — across twelve datasets harmonized by a three-level clinical ontology, a dedicated Spanish-language dataset validated by a practicing dermatologist (DermapixelAI 1.0), the Dermapixel R0 adaptation, and a multichannel clinical prototype in production.

01 — Thesis

Three contributions that travel together.

A · EVALUATION

A homogeneous and independent comparative evaluation

Up to fourteen foundation models with public weights evaluated in situ over a shared set of tasks — classification, segmentation, case retrieval, and text-guided zero-shot classification — and over datasets harmonized by a clinical L1/L2/L3 ontology, with an audit of the overlap between the pretraining corpus and the evaluation sets. There is no universally superior model: there is task-level specialization.

B · DATA

DermapixelAI 1.0: a Spanish-language clinical dataset

A Spanish-language clinical dataset with narrative text associated with each case (1,089 images, 672 cases with an image), delivered with formal datasheet-style documentation and a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license. Built on Dr. Taberner's Dermapixel archive, with a hierarchical L1/L2/L3 ontology mapped to SNOMED CT and ICD-10 and with expert validation of the diagnosis.

C · ADAPTATION

Dermapixel R0 and concept-based interpretability

A dedicated adaptation showing that a foundation model can be specialized to Spanish with modest resources: a supervised LoRA L2 branch (deployed as module M9) and a Spanish-language contrastive branch (SpanDerm-CLIP) that honestly documents its limits — not deployed because it does not generalize in real use. It is complemented with Sparse Autoencoder-based interpretability and a dictionary of clinical concepts defined together with Dr. Taberner.

02 — Prototype

DermApIxel: eleven AI modules in production.

An end-to-end clinical application in production at dermapixel.eu: eleven modules (M1–M11) plus the M4-bis search engine, a multichannel conversational assistant (web, WhatsApp, and voice), and a selector for three diagnostic modes (deterministic, AI-assisted hybrid, and maximum AI interpretation) over a single clinical safety net.

Awarded the AEDV Innovation and Artificial Intelligence Grant (€10,000, 53rd National Congress of Dermatology and Venereology, Maspalomas, May 2026) for its rollout into production.

05 — Collaborations

A four-way effort.

i
Academic supervision
UIB

Dr. Javier Varona Gómez supervises the thesis at the Escola Politècnica Superior of the Universitat de les Illes Balears.

ii
Clinical validation
Son Llàtzer Hosp.

Dr. Rosa Taberner, a dermatologist at Hospital Universitari Son Llàtzer and author of the Dermapixel blog, builds the hierarchical ontology together with the author, validates the diagnoses of the DermapixelAI 1.0 dataset, and is the reference clinical evaluator of the production prototype.

iii
International line
Monash

Dr. Siyuan Yan (Monash University, Melbourne), author of PanDerm, DermLIP, and DermFM-Zero, collaborates with the author on extending the work to Spanish-speaking casuistry. A continuation line beyond the thesis.

iv
Second source (future)
Dr. A. Llambrich

Dr. Alex Llambrich contributes his teaching archive (clinical and dermoscopic photography) as a second source of real data to strengthen the long tail of rare diagnoses. The Alex Llambrich dataset is provided for training only and —at the author's request— remains private: it will not be published. Its diagnoses are candidates pending expert validation. A continuation line beyond the thesis.
